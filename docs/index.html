How the verdict is decided the model, its training data, its calibration

A paired model, because persuasion is relative. The model never scores a draft on its own. It was trained on pairs of replies to the same ChangeMyView post where one reply changed the poster's mind (they awarded a delta) and the other did not, the design of Tan, Niculae, Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil & Lee, Winning Arguments (WWW 2016). Comparing two answers to the same view controls the topic, which is what makes the comparison meaningful.

What it reads. Twelve plain features per draft (length, how much it covers the poster's points, whether it argues in its own words or echoes theirs, direct address, questions, examples, numbers, hedging, links, acknowledgement, sentence length), listed with the learned weights in "The full numbers". A logistic regression on the difference between the two drafts' features gives a probability, recalibrated on held-out data (Platt scaling) so that "70%" means what it says as closely as the data allows.

Training and the frozen split. 4,263 pairs from the authors' Winning Arguments corpus (r/ChangeMyView, 2013–2015), split by conversation using the corpus's own train/holdout flag: 2,772 pairs fitted the model, 684 calibrated it, and 807 were held out and touched once, for the numbers on this page. The plan was frozen before training (PLAN.md in the repository).

Calibration on the held-out pairs

Read it as: when the model puts a draft in the 70–80% band, that draft actually won about as often as the band says. Bands at the extremes hold few pairs, so they are noisier. Overall the predictions are off by about 4 points on average (expected calibration error 0.035).

Limits. The model learned from one community's arguments, in 2013–2015, in one format (a written reply to a stated view). It reads surface features only: it cannot tell whether a claim is true, and a draft can game it. Treat the verdict as a second reader with known blind spots, not as a judge.

The training write-up, weights and per-pair held-out predictions are in the repository's Research notes.
